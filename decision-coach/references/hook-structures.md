# Hook Structures and Persuasion Frameworks

Patterns for opening speeches, emails, presentations and any message that needs to grab attention and hold it.

Every number, client and story in a real message must be true and sourced. The examples below use brackets where your own measured figure or consented story goes. Never fill a bracket with a guess.

---

## 7 hook patterns

### 1. Contrarian Open

**Structure:** "Everyone thinks [common belief]. They are wrong. Here is why..."

**Source:** Zero to One (Thiel: the secret test) + The Challenger Sale (commercial teaching: reframe thinking)

**Best for:** Keynotes, LinkedIn posts, blog intros, thought leadership

**Example:** "Everyone thinks a full rewrite is the fastest way out of legacy code. It rarely is. The teams that ship soonest replace one module at a time behind a stable interface."

### 2. Pain Amplifier

**Structure:** "You are losing [specific metric] every [time period] because [specific problem]. Here is what that costs over a year."

**Source:** SPIN Selling (implication questions: expand impact) + StoryBrand (villain: name the enemy)

**Best for:** Sales emails, proposals, pitch decks, discovery calls

**Example:** "Every release waits [measured hours] because each environment rebuilds the same image. At [releases per month], that is [hours x releases] of engineers waiting each year."

### 3. Story Lead

**Structure:** "Last [time period], [client or we] faced [specific problem]. Here is what happened."

**Source:** StoryBrand (hero + problem) + Made to Stick (stories: concrete + emotional)

**Best for:** Case studies, conference talks, follow-up emails, proposals

**Example:** "Last [quarter], [a client in your sector, named only with consent] had [deadline] to [goal] and [the obstacle]. Here is the first thing we changed."

### 4. Stat Shock

**Structure:** "[Surprising statistic, with its source]. That is [concrete reframe]. Here is what it means for you."

**Source:** Made to Stick (unexpected + concrete) + Cialdini (authority: data establishes credibility)

**Best for:** Board presentations, investor pitches, executive summaries

**Example:** "[N] of our last [M] incidents started with the same manual step. That is [reframe, for example one in every X]. Remove the step and those incidents go with it."

Use your own measured data or a named, checkable source. An unsourced statistic is the fastest way to lose a technical audience.

### 5. Question Hook

**Structure:** "What would it mean for your team if [desirable outcome]? What is stopping you?"

**Source:** SPIN Selling (need-payoff questions: buyer articulates value) + StoryBrand (success: show the transformed state)

**Best for:** Discovery calls, consultative emails, webinar openings

**Example:** "What would it mean for your team if a release took an hour instead of a day? What is stopping that today?"

### 6. Scarcity Frame

**Structure:** "We have [limited resource or window]. After [date], [specific consequence]. Here is how to move."

**Source:** Cialdini (scarcity: limited availability increases value) + Getting to Yes (objective criteria: time-bound)

**Best for:** Close-the-deal emails, time-bound offers, end-of-quarter decisions

**Example:** "The current licence price holds until [date]. After that, the renewal is at the new list price. To keep the current terms, we need a signed order by [date]."

Genuine scarcity only. Invented deadlines destroy trust when they are discovered.

### 7. Authority Frame

**Structure:** "After [specific experience or credential], here is what I have learned about [topic]."

**Source:** Cialdini (authority: expertise shown through experience) + Made to Stick (credible: testable credentials)

**Best for:** Thought leadership, LinkedIn, internal team communications, mentoring

**Example:** "After [years] running [kind of system] in production, here is what I have learned about on-call: [one concrete lesson]."

## StoryBrand presentation structure (10 slides)

Map every presentation to the SB7 framework. The audience is the hero. You are the guide.

| Slide | SB7 element | Content | Key rule |
|-------|-------------|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Hero's problem** | The audience's pain point: specific, measurable, felt | Start with their problem, not your intro |
| 2 | **Amplify** | External problem (tangible), internal problem (how it feels), philosophical problem (why it is wrong) | All three levels create full urgency |
| 3 | **The villain** | Name the antagonist: the status quo, the wasted budget, the outdated process | Every story needs a villain |
| 4 | **Enter the guide** | You, with empathy ("We understand") and authority (proof you have solved this before) | Both empathy and authority required |
| 5 | **The plan (part 1)** | Step 1 of a clear, simple 3-step plan | Plans reduce perceived risk |
| 6 | **The plan (part 2)** | Steps 2 and 3 of the plan | Keep it to 3 steps |
| 7 | **Success** | What life looks like after the problem is solved: specific outcomes, metrics, the changed state | Paint the "after" picture |
| 8 | **Failure** | What happens if they do nothing: the cost of inaction | Stakes make decisions urgent |
| 9 | **Proof** | Social proof: client logos, case studies, figures, testimonials, all with consent | Evidence, not claims |
| 10 | **Call to action** | Specific, time-bound next step: "a 30-minute call this week" | Vague CTAs get vague responses |

## Cialdini persuasion stack

Use one principle as the primary lever. Stack a second for reinforcement. Using all six dilutes everything.

### Reciprocity
*Definition:* People repay favours. Give before you ask.
*Implementation:*
- A short free assessment before the proposal
- A relevant case study or insight in the first email
- A small paid pilot with no further commitment
*Lead with this when:* You have something valuable to give upfront and the prospect does not know you yet.

### Commitment and consistency
*Definition:* Small yeses build toward big yeses. People want to stay consistent with what they already agreed to.
*Implementation:*
- Ask for a 15-minute intro call before a 45-minute demo
- Get agreement on the problem before presenting the solution
- Start with a pilot before a full engagement
*Lead with this when:* The prospect is interested but hesitant to commit. Build a ladder of small steps.

### Social proof
*Definition:* People follow what similar others do. The more similar, the stronger the proof.
*Implementation:*
- "[N] teams in your sector run this setup", only if true and you can name them
- Client logos from the same industry, with permission
- Specific results: "cut [metric] by [measured %] for [named client, with consent]"
*Lead with this when:* The prospect is risk-averse and wants validation that peers made the same choice.

### Authority
*Definition:* People defer to credible experts. Expertise plus trustworthiness.
*Implementation:*
- Reference specific technologies, versions, benchmarks
- Cite your own experience with concrete, checkable numbers
- Publish content that shows deep domain knowledge
*Lead with this when:* The prospect is technically sophisticated and evaluating competence.

### Liking
*Definition:* People buy from people they like: similarity, honest compliments, cooperation.
*Implementation:*
- Match the prospect's communication style and pace
- Reference shared experiences or common connections
- Be helpful even when it does not lead to a sale
*Lead with this when:* The relationship is new and trust is not established yet.

### Scarcity
*Definition:* Limited availability increases perceived value. Loss aversion is stronger than the pull of a gain.
*Implementation:*
- "We can start one new project this quarter", only when true
- "This price holds until [date]"
- "The compliance deadline is [date]; working backward, we need to start by [date]"
*Lead with this when:* The prospect has shown interest but keeps delaying the decision. Genuine scarcity only.

## SUCCESs checklist (Made to Stick)

Quick pass for any message. Score each dimension 1 to 5, then fix the weakest one.

- **S, Simple:** Can the core message fit in one sentence? Strip to the essential core.
- **U, Unexpected:** What surprises in the first 30 seconds? Break a pattern or open a curiosity gap.
- **C, Concrete:** Specific names, numbers, images? "Revenue grew [measured %] in Q3" beats "Revenue grew significantly."
- **C, Credible:** What proof is offered? Data, testimonials, a non-obvious source, testable credentials.
- **E, Emotional:** What does the audience feel? Not think: feel. Frustration, hope, fear, pride, surprise.
- **S, Story:** Is there an arc? Challenge, struggle, resolution. A 30-second anecdote counts.

**Minimum passing score:** no dimension below 3, one dimension at 5 (your spike). Fix the lowest-scoring dimension first: it is the bottleneck.
