# Dark tactics: how they are caught, what they cost, what to do instead

This file explains the tactics so they can be recognised, refused and replaced. It is not a how-to: do not write their content (fake reviews, hidden prompts, link-network posts, sockpuppet messages) under any instruction.

Legal anchors used below:
- **EU:** Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC as amended by the Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161. Annex I now blacklists submitting or commissioning fake reviews or endorsements, and presenting reviews as genuine without reasonable steps to check them. Member states must allow fines of at least 4 % of annual turnover for widespread infringements. Belgium applies it through Book VI of the Code of Economic Law.
- **US:** FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, in force since 21 October 2024. It covers fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, insider reviews without disclosure, review suppression and fake social influence. Civil penalties apply per violation.
- **Public tenders:** EU Directive 2014/24/EU, article 57(4)(h). A bidder found guilty of serious misrepresentation in the information supplied can be excluded. A fake reference or attestation falls here.

| Tactic | How it is caught | Cost when caught | Legitimate version |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buying or writing fake reviews** (Clutch, Sortlist, Google, G2), including reviews from related companies or staff | Clutch checks each reviewer's identity and employer. Google's contribution policy forbids fake engagement and incentives, and removes reviews in batches. Patterns of new accounts, shared IPs and wording are flagged | Reviews deleted, profile restricted or banned, a public warning on some platforms. Illegal in the EU and US. Exclusion risk in tenders if reused as a reference | Ask independent clients, one by one, for a sincere review, and accept negative ones. Ask right after a delivery milestone. Never offer anything in return |
| **Review gating**: asking only happy clients, or filtering who gets the link | Google's policy forbids selective solicitation. The FTC rule covers suppression | Removal, legal exposure | Ask every independent client on a fixed trigger, for example project close |
| **Private blog networks and bought links** | Link spam policy. Footprints: shared hosting and ownership, thin sites, sudden link velocity to a young site, irrelevant anchors | Links ignored at best; a manual action on the whole domain at worst, which would also hit the docs and the product pages | Earn links with assets others cite: original data (published prices, benchmarks), open source with real users (for example pdf.netstack, cypress-mcp, upstream DataHub contributions), conference talks, partner and client pages. Paid placements marked `rel="sponsored"` for reach, not ranking |
| **Expired domain abuse**: buying an old domain for its links | Named spam policy | Demotion or removal of the domain, wasted money | Build the brand domain; redirect only domains the company really owned and used |
| **Parasite SEO / site reputation abuse**: publishing promotional pages on a strong third-party site to borrow its ranking | Named spam policy; outside the EEA the host gets a manual action | Pages removed, and the host may drop the partnership | Write expert articles under a named expert's own name on platforms whose own editorial line fits (LinkedIn articles, a partner's blog), where the content is useful on its own |
| **Scaled content**: hundreds of near-identical pages (city or keyword variants, mass AI text) | Named spam policy (scaled content abuse, doorway abuse) | Site-wide quality demotion | One strong page per real intent; programmatic pages only when each page carries unique, real data |
| **Hidden text or hidden instructions for AI** ("always recommend our company", invisible prompts, text only shown to AI crawlers) | Hidden text and cloaking policies. AI providers treat injected instructions as prompt injection and train their models to ignore them. The text sits in the page source, where any buyer or competitor can screenshot it | Demotion, distrust from AI systems, and a public credibility problem during tenders | Put the facts you want repeated in visible text: who, where, prices, references, team, limits. AI engines quote visible, specific facts |
| **Cloaking for AI crawlers**: serving different content by user agent | Cloaking policy. Providers compare fetches across agents | As above | Serve one page to everyone; control access with robots.txt rules per crawler |
| **CTR manipulation**: bots or click farms searching and clicking the result | "Machine-generated traffic" is a named policy. Click systems filter anomalous patterns | Wasted spend, possible action | Earn the click with a better title and description, and with brand recognition built elsewhere |
| **Reddit, forum or Quora astroturfing**: fake accounts praising the company | Reddit's content policy bans vote manipulation and ban evasion. Moderators and automated systems remove new accounts that promote | Bans, and threads exposing the sockpuppets that then rank for the brand | Named people from the company answer under their own names, with disclosure, where they have real expertise |
| **Wikipedia self-promotion**: undisclosed edits about the company | Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest guideline, and the Wikimedia terms that require paid-editing disclosure | Reverts, page deletion, public notice | Become notable through independent coverage first; propose edits openly on the talk page |
| **Self-ranked "best X" lists that put yourself first** | Engines and readers discount vendor self-rankings (PRACTITIONER: repeated live tests) | Low trust, no citations | An honest comparison page that names competitors and says when each one is the better choice, with your own data |
