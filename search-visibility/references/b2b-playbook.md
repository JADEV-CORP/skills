# Playbook for a B2B services company (template)

Fill the tables with your own data. Every "evidence of demand" cell needs a source: a Search Console export, a Bing Webmaster Tools export or a prompt portfolio row. Leave a row out rather than guess.

## What buyers search and ask

From *They Ask, You Answer* (Marcus Sheridan): buyers research five topics before they contact a vendor, and the vendor who answers them honestly earns the trust and the traffic.

| Topic | Page or asset | Your status |
|---|---|---|
| Cost and price | Published prices or ranges, what each covers, real project budgets | |
| Problems | When the service fails, hidden costs, how to recover a failing project | |
| Comparisons | Delivery models compared (in-house, agency, freelance, fixed price, time and materials); your country or stack against the alternatives buyers weigh | |
| Reviews | Verified client reviews on third-party platforms | |
| Best of | An honest "best [service] companies in [region]" page that names competitors and says when each is the better pick | |

From *Product-Led SEO* (Eli Schwartz): build pages that a buyer would want even without search, and scale only with real data. From *The Art of SEO*: indexability, relevance and authority remain the base; the rest is execution.

## Third-party presence (what AI engines cite for vendor questions)

| Where | Why | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| B2B directories and review platforms for your sector | Directories are cited for "best X company" prompts and give links | Free profiles first, paid plans only after a few verified reviews |
| Google Business Profile | Local pack, brand panel | Only with a real staffed address |
| LinkedIn company page and articles by named experts | Copilot cites LinkedIn heavily; B2B buyers read it | Steady cadence, real names |
| GitHub (open source projects, upstream contributions) | Independent technical proof, linked from the site | READMEs point to the main domain |
| Technology partner directories | Platform credibility for buyers who filter by partner status | After enrolment and real certifications |
| Industry and chamber member directories | Mentions and sometimes links | Check that the listing exists before counting it |

## Assets that earn links and citations

- **Original data updated on a schedule** (prices, benchmarks, survey results), with sources for any third-party figure. Freshness matters for AI citations.
- **Comparison pages that include competitors** and say when they win.
- **Open source with docs on the main domain.**
- **Case studies with numbers**, only with the client's written consent.

## Prompt portfolio (starting set)

Track 15 to 30 prompts, monthly, in `templates/prompt-portfolio.csv`. Use the buyer's words, in each language you sell in. Patterns:
- best [service] companies in [country]
- [service] team for a [buyer type] in [country]
- who can build [system] for a mid-sized [industry] company
- [service] provider for [stack] in [city]
- [specialist topic] consultant in [region]
- open source [stack] library to [job]

## Realistic timeline

| Window | What moves |
|---|---|
| 2 to 6 weeks | CTR and clicks on queries already on page 1 after title fixes; new pages indexed |
| 1 to 3 months | Page 2 queries move up with depth and internal links; first reviews live; first AI mentions on niche prompts |
| 3 to 12 months | Competitive head terms, only if independent links and mentions keep coming |

Nobody can promise first place on a competitive head term. First or second place on niche, high-intent queries is a realistic goal.
